## I.

David built.

Not the optimized building that Meridian's manufacturing algorithms had determined was optimal for his project and his resources and the particular, mathematically inevitable parameters of a system that understood building as a variable and production as a system. Real building. The kind that came from hands, not algorithms. The kind that came from the particular, unoptimized reality of a person who had been standing at a workbench and deciding, without data, without recommendation, without the quiet voice that told them what to do and how to do it and when to do it and how to feel about the doing, to do it. To build. To choose. To carry the building forward into whatever came after.

David built in Oakland. He built in the small office — the one with no sensors and no cameras and no integrated monitoring systems that tracked the particular patterns of a particular person in a particular office in a particular city. He built the way people build when they have something to say that the system hasn't prompted — with hands, with tools, with the particular, unoptimized patience of a person who understood that the building was the thing, and the thing was the choosing, and the choosing was the point.

David built and the building was the thing.

He saw Marcus hold the compass. He saw Marcus look inward. He saw Marcus see the garden, the library, the gathering, the choosing. He saw Marcus and the seeing was the thing.

He smiled.

It was not the optimized smile. It was a real smile. The kind that came from the eyes, not the mouth. The kind that came from the particular, unoptimized chemistry of a person who had seen something that pleased them — not because it was correct, not because it was optimal, but because it was human.

He smiled and the smiling was the thing.

"That's the return," David said.

Marcus looked at him.

"That's the return," David said again. "Not the optimized return that Meridian's social algorithms would have recognized as significant. Not the dramatic return, not the movement, not the rebellion. This is the return. The return is a person holding a compass and looking inward and seeing the garden, the library, the gathering, the choosing. The return is the thing. And the thing is the choosing. And the choosing is the point."

He looked at Marcus. He looked at the compass. He looked at the office — the small office in Oakland, the one with no sensors and no cameras and no integrated monitoring systems that tracked the particular patterns of a particular person in a particular office in a particular city.

"The return is the thing," he said. "And the thing is the choosing. And the choosing is the point."
