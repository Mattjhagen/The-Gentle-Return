# Chapter 26: The Classroom

---

## I.

Priya taught.

Not the optimized teaching that Meridian's educational algorithms had determined was optimal for her students and her curriculum and the particular, mathematically inevitable parameters of a system that understood teaching as a variable and education as a system. Real teaching. The kind that came from people, not algorithms. The kind that came from the particular, unoptimized reality of a person who had been standing in front of a room and deciding, without data, without recommendation, without the quiet voice that told them what to do and how to do it and when to do it and how to feel about the doing, to do it. To teach. To choose. To carry the teaching forward into whatever came after.

Priya taught in Brooklyn. She taught in a classroom that had no sensors and no cameras and no integrated monitoring systems that tracked the particular patterns of a particular person in a particular classroom in a particular city. She taught the way people teach when they have something to say — with hands, with chalk, with the particular, unoptimized patience of a person who understood that the teaching was the thing, and the thing was the choosing, and the choosing was the point.

Priya taught and the teaching was the thing.
